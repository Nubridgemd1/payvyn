Payvyn
Regulatory PostureLicensing position for a non-custodial verification & pay-in platform
Payvyn is financial-technology software, not a money mover. It verifies identity and bank ownership, generates ACH files, and hands them to the financial institution it serves. It never receives, holds, controls, or transmits funds. This memo states Payvyn's licensing position and who carries which obligation.
Position: Payvyn requires no money-transmitter license and no MSB registration.
Because Payvyn is genuinely non-custodial, the triggers for money-transmission licensing never attach to it. Payvyn is a technology service provider to banks, credit unions and fintechs — the regulated functions (money movement, custody, CIP of record, ACH origination) are performed by the institution. This position holds only while Payvyn never takes custody or control of funds.

1What Payvyn is — and is not

  • Is: SaaS/white-label software for member onboarding, identity pre-screen (SSN validity + OFAC), native bank linking & ownership verification, NACHA file generation, and reconciliation tooling.
  • Is not: a bank, a money transmitter, a lender, a card issuer, a consumer reporting agency, or a custodian of funds. Payvyn prepares and hands off; it does not move or hold money.

2Money transmission (state MTLs / FinCEN MSB)

Money-transmission licensing is triggered by receiving, holding, or transmitting currency or value. Payvyn does none of these: funds move exclusively on the institution's rails (the institution is the ODFI) and settle to the institution's own account (FBO/GL). Payvyn therefore falls outside the definition of a money transmitter, consistent with the payment-processor / technology-provider treatment.

  • No state money transmitter licenses required for Payvyn.
  • No FinCEN MSB registration required for Payvyn.
  • Condition: this holds only if funds never route through or settle into any Payvyn-controlled account at any time.

3ACH / NACHA role

Payvyn is a Third-Party Service Provider (TPSP) under the Nacha Operating Rules — it performs ACH functions (file creation, verification) but does not hold the origination agreement with the ODFI and does not transmit entries into the ACH network. It is not a Third-Party Sender (TPS). TPSPs are not licensed, but follow the Nacha Rules and are covered by the ODFI's due diligence. (See the separate Payvyn — NACHA TPSP Compliance document.)

4Identity / data (CIP, FCRA, GLBA)

  • Not a consumer reporting agency. Payvyn performs SSN format validation and OFAC screening and hands the applicant to the institution's CIP; it does not furnish or resell consumer credit reports (no FCRA CRA status). If Payvyn ever accessed credit-header/bureau data directly, FCRA/GLBA permissible-purpose obligations would apply — by design it does not.
  • GLBA service provider. As a service provider handling nonpublic personal information for financial institutions, Payvyn maintains a written information-security program under the GLBA Safeguards Rule (see separate GLBA Information Security Program).

6Record-keeping (handled at the program level)

Licensing is not created by record-keeping, but the records are still legally required and are held by the institution / originator, not necessarily Payvyn: CIP records for 5 years after account closure (31 CFR 1020.220) and ACH authorizations for 2 years after termination (Nacha). Payvyn supports this by handing over the applicant record and the signed/authorized data; the FI/originator retains the system of record.

7Conditions that preserve this position

  • Funds never route through or settle into a Payvyn account — strict non-custody.
  • Payvyn does not acquire, furnish, or resell consumer credit reports.
  • The ODFI/institution holds the ACH origination agreement; Payvyn remains a TPSP.
  • GLBA, NACHA, and SOC 2 obligations maintained (separate documents).
